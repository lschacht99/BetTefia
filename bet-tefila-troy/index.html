Why it matters

Not a museum. Not just a building.

This is a protected historic place with an active Jewish future: minyanim, holiday meals, students, family programs, learning, and visitor support right in downtown Troy.

History

Built to last

The River Street building was constructed in 1909 for a larger Troy Jewish community and still carries its monumental dome, barrel-vaulted sanctuary, and balcony.

Community

Alive now

Shabbat tables, kosher meals, student nights, holiday programs, and family-friendly events make the shul a living address for Jewish connection.

Travelers

A stop on the way

If you are traveling through Troy, visiting RPI, exploring genealogy, or need kosher logistics, start here and someone can help you plan.

1850

Original congregation roots are recorded in Troy Jewish history.

1909

2016

The synagogue is listed on the National Register of Historic Places as part of Troy’s historic fabric.

Today

Orthodox / Chabad Jewish life continues through prayer, learning, food, outreach, and community events.
