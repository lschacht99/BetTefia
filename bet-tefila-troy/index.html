The Troy story

Built for a larger Jewish Troy. Ready for today.

Beth Tephilah means “House of Prayer.” Synagogues360 describes it as a grand historic synagogue overlooking the Hudson River, founded by a congregation established in 1850 and housed in this 1909 building. The site keeps that story visible and immediately useful.

A warmer historic palette.

The visual direction uses oak and parchment warmth, copper accents for the dome, and blue-gray sanctuary tones instead of a cold institutional look. It feels old, cared-for, and alive.

Connected to campus and community

River Street, downtown Troy, RPI, Sage, and Jewish travelers.

Beth Tephilah sits by downtown Troy, near the Hudson River and Russell Sage College, with RPI close by. The site now speaks to students, families, work travelers, alumni, and visitors who need a Jewish place to land.

Leadership & hospitality

Make the human welcome clear.

Public directories connect Beth Tephilah and Troy Chabad with Rabbi Leible Morrison and Mrs. Elisheva Morrison. The website points people to call or write first so current times, meals, events, and logistics are confirmed by the people on the ground.
